Week 3 Project · From answers to actions

Chat or call,
Pebby's there to help.

Book, change or cancel, any time, in a few words.

Last week Pebby answered questions. This week it acts: it books appointments on the spa's real calendars, takes calls on a real phone number, and hears feedback. The AI talks and decides; code does everything exact, and nothing happens without the customer's yes.

The idea

An assistant that does things has to be safe to trust

Answering "how much is a facial?" wrongly is a bad answer. Booking the wrong facial on the wrong day is a real appointment a therapist turns up for. Once Pebby could act, the question changed from "is the answer right?" to "can the AI ever do something the customer didn't ask for?"

Our answer: an AI only where thinking is needed, which is reading what people mean. Everything exact (dates, times, availability, booking) is code. The service, day, time and therapist always come from the customer's own words, and every change waits for their yes.

What we built

One Pebby, on the website and the phone

Customers

  • Book one service or several in one go, with one yes; packages book every service they include
  • Add-ons (a scalp massage with a facial), also onto a booking they already have
  • Change a booking ("same time, deep tissue instead") or cancel one or more
  • Ask which days work, and when someone is free
  • Call the spa's phone number and talk to the same Pebby
  • Share feedback, and get a caring reply

The owner

  • Every booking lands on the therapist's own Google Calendar, with its package and add-ons
  • A Feedback tab: one card per piece of feedback, complaints flagged, closed with a note
  • Sign-in, and a permission for each owner-only page and tool
  • A phone-line test widget, and a debug panel that shows every step of a turn

The agents

Two AI agents, and code for everything exact

An agent is an AI that decides what to do next, for example which tool to use, instead of following a fixed script. We gave that role only to jobs that need judgement over language.

AI agent

Pebby, the front desk

  • The only one who talks to the customer
  • Decides what each message is about: a question, a booking, feedback, or something for the staff
  • Collects a booking's details in conversation, then sends them once
  • Tools: search the guide, booking, record feedback, hand off to staff
AI agent

The Feedback agent

  • Reads how the customer feels and what about (treatment, staff, facilities…)
  • Writes the reply they see, kind and careful: no promises of refunds, no invented facts
  • Asks one question when the feedback is too brief to act on
  • Files a card for the owner, open until it is closed
Code

The booking side

  • Details: checks every detail is in the customer's words, reads "Thursday, after 3pm" into a date and times
  • The yes: reads the details back, then the times found
  • Booking: fits the services back to back on the day
  • Executor: books all of it or none, never twice

The phone line

ElevenLabs and Twilio: the same Pebby, by voice

Twilio

Provides the spa's real phone number and carries the call. When someone dials it, Twilio connects the call to ElevenLabs.

ElevenLabs

Runs the conversation by voice: turns the caller's speech into text, sends it to Pebby, speaks Pebby's reply in a natural voice, and handles turn-taking. Its "custom model" setting points at our server, so the thinking stays ours.

Our server

Receives each turn as an OpenAI-style chat request, protected by a secret, and streams the reply back. It runs exactly the same Pebby, tools and rules as the chat; only the wording changes for speech.

  • Caller ID as the phone number: a caller is only asked for their name, and a returning caller is asked whether it's about their upcoming booking.
  • Spoken replies: "three p.m.", "Friday the thirtieth", a reference read letter by letter, at most three options.
  • No silence, no dropped calls: a short filler if the reply takes over 1.5 s, the handover message if a turn would pass 8 s, and never an empty reply.
  • Callers who keep talking: ElevenLabs sends a longer version of the same turn; we run one turn at a time and undo the replaced one, so Pebby never remembers a reply the caller didn't hear.

How it behaves

Six principles, enforced in code

Every rule lives in one file, rules.py, and is enforced by code, not by asking the model nicely. A new situation fits one of these instead of getting a rule of its own.

  1. Nothing the customer says is dropped

    Name and phone first; anything asked meanwhile is answered right after.

  2. Every detail is the customer's own

    A service, day, time, therapist or booking a model filled in is dropped, and the customer is asked.

  3. No change without a yes

    Code writes each question: the details read back, the plan found, what a change cancels.

  4. All or nothing, never twice

    A plan is re-checked at the yes and booked whole or not at all.

  5. Bounded work, then a person

    Loops, failures, bulk or recurring requests: the staff are told, and the customer always gets an answer.

  6. A model only where thinking is needed

    Pebby and the Feedback agent read language; dates, times and booking are code.

What's done so far

From answering to acting, one problem at a time

  1. Step 1

    Pebby books, with tools

    • A booking agent (LangGraph) with tools to find times, book, list and cancel
    • Bookings on Google Calendar, one calendar per therapist
    Observation
    Booking from the chat worked
    Issue
    The AI picked the exact time itself, and booked one service at a time

    Example: a three-service package needed three separate yeses, and could end half-booked.

    Fix
    Booking rules in code, and the customer's yes before anything is booked or cancelled
  2. Step 2

    The phone line

    • Twilio number → ElevenLabs voice agent → our server, running the same Pebby
    Observation
    Real calls worked end to end, with caller ID
    Issue
    ElevenLabs waits 10 seconds for a reply, and a caller who keeps talking sends overlapping turns

    Example: an early reply the caller never heard was still remembered, and led to an offer for the wrong day.

    Fix
    One turn at a time, replaced turns undone, a filler, never an empty reply, the handover on any failure
  3. Step 3

    Access checks

    • Roles, permissions, owner sign-in, tools offered by role
    Observation
    The two chats were kept apart only by what each could search
    Issue
    Nothing in code stopped a role reaching another's data
    Fix
    Explicit permissions on every tool and route, with tests
  4. Step 4

    A second AI to plan bookings

    • A Planner AI turned a request into a plan; one yes booked it all, or none
    Observation
    One yes for a whole package
    Issue
    It invented services and picked days on its own, and took 8 to 20 model calls

    Example: asked for the Bridal Glow Prep Package, it planned a Signature facial and a body scrub, on a day the customer never chose. One plan took 11.7 s: too slow for the phone.

    Fix
    Fitting services into a day is arithmetic, so it became code: instant, and it cannot invent anything
  5. Step 5

    Every detail is the customer's own

    • Pebby passes the customer's own words; code checks they were said, then reads them into dates and times
    • The details are read back before any times are looked for
    Observation
    The AI kept filling in what the customer hadn't given
    Issue
    Instructions alone didn't stop it

    Examples: "today, after 09:34" copied from the clock; all five facials for "a facial"; a therapist nobody asked for; the name "answer".

    Fix
    One check in code, "picked out by the customer's words", for services, bookings, therapists, days, times and the name
  6. Step 6

    Making the open model reliable

    • Pebby runs on a cheaper open model (Qwen) or Claude, by one setting
    Observation
    Qwen repeated the same tool call, then the staff took over
    Issue
    It was forced to call a tool on every turn; its replies were stored as unanswered tool calls; sometimes it wrote a tool call as text

    Example: the same guide search three times in one turn, for "facial hair wax".

    Fix
    No forced tool call; an answer is required only when there is nothing else to call; written tool calls are read; replies are stored as text
  7. Step 7

    Simplified

    • The Planner and a second booking AI removed: booking is code
    • One folder of 13 files, read in the order a turn runs
    Observation
    20 files in three folders, and a booking AI that almost never ran
    Issue
    Hard to follow and hard to monitor
    Fix
    Six principles in one file, and a debug panel that shows every step as a flow
  8. Step 8 · Now

    The Feedback agent

    • Reads feedback, writes the reply, asks once when it is too brief, files a card the owner closes
    Observation
    Feedback is real language work: "the massage was great but the room was freezing" is mixed, about the facilities, and worth a call
    Result
    A second agent with a job of its own, and a Feedback tab for the owner
